# portraits-from-my-jungle
## My Strategies for training the models
My strategy was to maintain an open session in Colab to reduce problems with the installs and dependencies. I got animal image datasets from Huggingface, used Google Drive to organize my training data, and put it together in Colab during runtime. I organized my colab cells to separate the pip installs, setup cells, training and generations. I wanted to make sure I saved the generations and or training to my drive, to avoid losing everything if my session closed or something happened. 
## How the fictional beasts’ images sounds and language was generated:
### Image generation:
Two datasets of animals labeled by species were combined and prepared for the training. The Stable Diffusion v1-5 model was imported to Google Colab and trained with the datasets. Ater the training, 16 different animal names or combinations were given as prompts, and 3 variations were generated for each prompt. The process was repeated three times for a total of 144 generations. The 16 best results were kept, as well as their corresponding names/prompts for future reference and the sound and text generations.
### Sound generation:
The Stable Audio 3 small-music model was used to generate all of the sounds. For each of the chosen images, the image prompts were used as inspiration to describe the sounds of each imaginary animal. Three sound variations were created for each animal and the preferred generation for each was selected, a 120 second ambient jungle soundscape was also generated for continuous background noise.
### Language generation:
The text for each animal was generated by Codex based on the name/prompt of the animal, the model was asked to imagine how the sound of the animal in question would be written like.

## how the quality of the images and sounds could be improved:
Image-wise, I forgot to disable the NSFW filter which might be the reason I was getting some black images, I could've also reinstated my negative prompt, increased the training steps and the resolution.
For the sound, I believe increasing the amount of steps and variations of generations would be the best way to find higher quality results.
